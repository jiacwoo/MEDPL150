“In Plato's Cave” from On Photography by Susan Sontag

“Three, two, one—everyone smile! Oh what the heck please retake that…” The sound of laughter follows after somebody blurts it out while looking at the result. Photos getting retaken because people don’t like how they looked almost always happens. The funniest thing is, they don’t even look bad! Like I swear it’s always the people who look the best that complain about how bad they look. It’s really hard getting that perfect picture because people have many different opinions but after getting that one shot that everyone approves of is so rewarding.

I cherish every single picture I take but group pictures will always be one of my favorite photos to look back at in my gallery. Sometimes I would scroll through my camera roll, looking at everyone's silly faces while posing and memories of that day would come rushing back. Imagine in the future you randomly get reminded that 10 years ago you and your friends randomly skipped school to go on a day vacation (true story!). The happiness that me and my friends shared will forever be engraved in this one image.

I can’t remember this exact line but it goes something like “people should focus on the present but never forget the past”. There's many interpretations of this but it’s basically saying how the experiences you have accumulated in the past are a part of you and even in the present, can’t be erased. I think photos are a very important part in preserving memories not even of people but memories of events that have shaped you as a person. To take a picture is the same as creating this small story in this frame, a “tiny element of another world: the image‐world that bids to outlast us all”(Sontag, 5). I really like how Sontag words this, talking about how this world would outlast the people taking it. This really brings up how humans age and how we will grow old and eventually disappear from this world. But unlike humans, photos don’t age. I mean physical photos can age and even break but the world inside the photo is preserved.

The one thing I really love about photography is that technology has evolved to be able to capture everything so flawlessly and easily. From the time Sontag wrote the essay in 1977 to now, so much has changed about photography such as better lenses, new devices, and the creation of social media. The invention of the phone put a camera in everyone's pocket where they could just carry it around and snap pictures whenever they wanted. Cameras have now changed to be able to adapt to things like light exposure, saturation, and even shutter speed. These can all impact a lot of what you can do with perceptions and interpretations. I think one of the biggest changes from back then is social media. The introduction of social media made it easy for people around the world to share photos and it really changed why people take photos. It’s not just for preserving memories but for a way to express to other people and create beautiful art. This is also why trends and aesthetics start appearing because many people will follow if they like what they see. Another big thing is editing has become normalized where people can edit a photo to their desired image. This creates an argument between authentic and “fake” art because why learn how to take photos when you can just learn how to edit them. People will always have opinions no matter what so what matters is if you are happy with your photos. Photography will always be interpreted differently from each viewer. How you want viewers to interpret your picture is based on how you choose to capture the image.
