Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It.

After taking three years of history classes in high school, the Cold War has always been a favorite unit of mine. The best description of the war is that it’s basically a battle of mind games mainly between America and the Soviet Union which is current day Russia. It wasn’t really a war where the two sides fought with weapons but rather ideologically in who can become the strongest. America was fighting to stop the idea of communism that the Soviets were trying to spread. This was after World War II where America introduced the nuclear bomb to the world. Two were used on Japan which ended up becoming the turning point in ending the war. The news that America was able to produce bombs of that level made everyone cautious of America who is now a big superpower. That was until it was discovered that the Soviet Union also knew how to make nuclear bombs.

As the Soviets rose up to also be a big superpower, the tensions between them and America grew. The Soviet Union was founded on the ideology of communism and they wanted to spread it across the world to create a society of no class and equality. America was against it and wanted to stop the Soviets but after the news that they also owned nuclear bombs, they couldn't just threaten the Soviets with weapons. So America created the Truman doctrine which established that America would help any country stop the spread of communism. This marked the start of the Cold War. Since the start of the Cold War, there was great fear of nuclear war where both sides would start firing nuclear missiles at each other. So America and the Soviet Union created the doctrine MAD to stop the use of nuclear weapons on both sides. MAD stands for Mutually Assured Destruction and its where the use of nuclear weapons would wipe out both sides so nobody wins. In the end, not a single nuclear missile was fired.
